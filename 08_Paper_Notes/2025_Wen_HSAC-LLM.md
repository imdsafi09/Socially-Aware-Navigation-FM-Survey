# HSAC-LLM: Socially-Aware Robot Navigation Enhanced by Bidirectional Natural Language Conversations (Wen et al., IROS 2025)

Page convention: PDF index (p.N); printed pp.15314–15321.

## A. Bibliographic
IROS 2025, DOI 10.1109/IROS60139.2025.11246738; NYU/NYUAD, UCLA, Harvard, Tsinghua. Project https://hsacllm.github.io/. Conference; peer-reviewed.

## B. Position — S4
Only corpus paper where language is used for **bidirectional human–robot negotiation** during navigation (robot asks pedestrian to stop/move; pedestrian requests robot behaviour). The LLM's motion-relevant role is weak; motion is learned DRL.

## C. Research problem
- **Problem:** passive avoidance (detours) or one-way signalling (beeping) are ambiguous/intrusive; propose proactive dialogue to resolve conflicts (p.1).
- **Contributions (p.2):** LLM-facilitated social navigation; HSAC-LLM framework; evaluation metric covering safety, efficiency, comfort; sim + real validation.

## D. Technical method
- Img PreNet: 3 conv + 3 max-pool on 180° laser grid + 3-channel pedestrian grid (location, speed) → 512-d S_i; S_p relative goal over 3 steps (9-d); Lang PreNet → S_c from pedestrian voice messages (architecture not described); State PreNet FC 512 (p.2–3).
- HSAC: actor outputs discrete distribution over a_c ∈ {none, stop, margin-right, margin-left} and Gaussian continuous (v, ω) = tanh(μ + εσ) (Eq.1); critic Q(s, μ, ω, a_c); policy π(a | [S_i, S_p], S_c) (Eq.2).
- Lang GenNet: GPT-4 converts a_c into natural-language utterances (p.3).
- Reward: r_goal (r_arrive 500; penalty ε1(d_safe − d_min) if d_min < d_safe = 1.0; r_collide −500), r_shaping ε2 = 200, r_step −5, r_interact (request/response aligned) (Eq.3–6, p.3–4).
- Training: 2D simulator (OpenCV) with IRVO pedestrians who comply with stop/margin requests; v ∈ [0, 0.6] m/s, ω ∈ [−0.9, 0.9] rad/s; pedestrian 0.5 m/s; reverse-request probability 0.5; 128,000 steps on i7-10750H + RTX 2070 (p.4).

## J. Experimental evidence
2D sim: 3 scenes (Square random; Hallway head-on; Crosswalk crossing), 500 tests each; baselines A2C, PPO, TD3 (same reward). Gazebo: human-controlled avatar via leg tracking, microphone/speaker; qualitative robot-/pedestrian-proactive cases (Fig.4). Real: Hiwonder JetAuto Pro, Jetson Nano, RPLIDAR A1, hallway, two qualitative scenarios (Fig.5) (p.4–6).
